Job Market Paper

How Reduced Fertility Shaped Grandparental Childcare: Evidence from China’s “Later, Longer, Fewer” Campaign

with Lauren Jones and Tansel Yilmazer

Draft available on request

Does having fewer children and grandchildren change how much grandparents care about their grandchildren? We study China’s “Later, Longer, Fewer” (LLF) campaign of the 1970s, which sharply reduced fertility before the One-Child Policy. Using variation in campaign intensity across provinces and birth cohorts and four waves of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS, 2011–2018), we find that women more exposed to the campaign had fewer children and fewer grandchildren yet were more likely to care for their grandchildren. One unit of exposure raises the probability of providing grandchild care by 2.4 percentage points (6 percent of the mean). The effect operates at the extensive margin (whether grandmothers provide care) rather than through hours of care. It runs through sons’ children and is largest in families whose first child, or first two children, are sons. Consistent with Becker–Barro altruism, the results show that fertility restrictions shape intergenerational support across three generations.

How Reduced Fertility Affects Eldercare Received in Old Age: Evidence from China’s “Later, Longer, Fewer” Campaign

with Tansel Yilmazer and Lauren Jones

Abstract

We study how reduced fertility affects old-age support, using China’s “Later, Longer, Fewer” (LLF) campaign of the 1970s as a source of exogenous variation in family size. Individuals with greater LLF exposure have fewer children, which may reduce the eldercare they receive later in life. Using province-by-cohort variation in campaign intensity and three waves of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (2013–2018), we find that the LLF campaign reduced the eldercare parents receive, especially fathers. Because eldercare questions are asked only of respondents who report ADL/IADL limitations, we address sample selection using Lee bounds.

Wage Rates, Childcare Costs, and Grandmothers’ Labor Market Outcomes

with Lauren Jones and Tansel Yilmazer

Summary

This paper examines how the birth of a grandchild affects grandmothers’ labor supply and how childcare prices and the wages of mothers and grandmothers jointly shape household time allocation between childcare and market work. The paper uses the genealogical structure of the Panel Study of Income Dynamics (PSID) and an event-study design around the birth of the first grandchild.

How the Age Gap between Spouses Shapes Mortality

with Tansel Yilmazer and Lauren Jones

Summary

This paper examines how the age gap between spouses relates to mortality and the mechanisms through health status and health behaviors. It uses China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) microdata and survival analysis, and addresses sample selection by including spouses who died before the survey baseline.

The Effect of China’s “Later, Longer, Fewer” Campaign on Human Capital Accumulation

with Lauren Jones and Tansel Yilmazer

Summary

This paper examines how the LLF campaign affected the educational attainment of the exposed generation, using CHARLS microdata and a cohort-based event-study design.
